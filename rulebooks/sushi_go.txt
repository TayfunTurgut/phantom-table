SUSHI GO!


OVERVIEW

Sushi Go! is a fast card-drafting game for 2 to 5 players. Over the course of
three rounds, players pass hands of sushi cards around the table, each keeping
one card per turn to build the most valuable meal. Different sushi combinations
score in different ways - some reward sets, some reward having the most of a
type, and some (pudding) are only settled at the very end of the game. Whoever
has the most points after three rounds and the final dessert scoring wins.

A full game consists of 3 rounds. Each round, hands shrink one card at a time
until every card has been placed. Cards you keep are scored at the end of each
round, except pudding, which is set aside and scored only once, at the end of
the whole game.


COMPONENTS

108 cards total:

- Tempura x14
- Sashimi x14
- Dumpling x14
- Maki Roll cards x26 total, made up of: 6 cards showing 1 maki-roll icon,
  12 cards showing 2 maki-roll icons, and 8 cards showing 3 maki-roll icons
  (54 maki icons in all)
- Salmon Nigiri x10 (base value 2)
- Squid Nigiri x5 (base value 3)
- Egg Nigiri x5 (base value 1)
- Wasabi x6
- Chopsticks x4
- Pudding x10

You also need a pencil and a piece of paper to keep score; these are not
included in the box.


SETUP

1. Shuffle all 108 cards together thoroughly.

2. Deal a hand to each player, face-down. The size of each hand depends on the
   number of players:
   - 2 players: deal 10 cards to each player.
   - 3 players: deal 9 cards to each player.
   - 4 players: deal 8 cards to each player.
   - 5 players: deal 7 cards to each player.

3. Each player holds their hand in their hands, kept secret from opponents.

4. Place all remaining cards in a single face-down draw pile in the center of
   the playing area.

5. Choose one player to be the scorekeeper, who records scores on the paper.

Each player has an empty play area in front of them at the start; collected
(face-up) cards will accumulate there during the round.


HOW A TURN WORKS

The game is played over 3 rounds, and each round is made up of a series of
simultaneous turns.

On every turn:

1. All players secretly choose one card from their current hand that they want
   to keep, and place it face-down in front of themselves.

2. Once everyone has chosen, all players reveal their chosen cards at the same
   time, placing them face-up in their own play area.

3. Each player then passes their entire remaining hand face-down to the player
   on their left.

4. Everyone picks up the new hand passed to them. This new hand is one card
   smaller than before. The next turn begins with this new, smaller hand.

Turns repeat this way, with hands getting smaller each time, until the hands
are used up.

Face-up cards you have revealed stay in your play area until the end of the
round, when they are scored. It helps to group cards of the same type together
as you collect them.

The final card: When a hand is down to its last single card, that card is
passed on as usual; the player receiving it simply places it face-up with the
rest of their collected cards (there is no choice to make, since only one card
remains). This last card is often not useful, but occasionally a player will be
forced to hand over something valuable.


ACTIONS AND CARD EFFECTS

Cards are scored at the end of each round (except pudding). Their effects and
values are as follows.

Maki Rolls. Each maki roll card shows 1, 2, or 3 maki-roll icons at its top.
Maki cards are not scored individually; instead, at the end of the round, each
player totals the icons across all their maki cards, and points are awarded by
comparison between players (see Scoring and Tiebreakers).

Tempura. A set of exactly 2 tempura cards scores 5 points. A single, unpaired
tempura card is worth nothing. You may score multiple sets of tempura in the
same round (each complete pair of 2 is worth 5). Leftover tempura that does not
complete a pair scores nothing.

Sashimi. A set of exactly 3 sashimi cards scores 10 points. A single sashimi,
or a set of only 2, is worth nothing. You may score multiple sets of 3 in the
same round (each complete set of 3 is worth 10). Leftover sashimi that does not
complete a set of 3 scores nothing.

Dumplings. The more dumpling cards you collect, the more points you score, on
an accelerating scale based on your total number of dumplings:
   - 1 dumpling = 1 point
   - 2 dumplings = 3 points
   - 3 dumplings = 6 points
   - 4 dumplings = 10 points
   - 5 or more dumplings = 15 points (15 is the maximum; additional dumplings
     beyond 5 add nothing)

Nigiri. Nigiri cards score a fixed value on their own:
   - Squid Nigiri = 3 points
   - Salmon Nigiri = 2 points
   - Egg Nigiri = 1 point

Wasabi. A wasabi card by itself scores nothing. Instead, wasabi triples the
value of the next nigiri card you play after it. When you choose and reveal a
squid, salmon, or egg nigiri and you already have a wasabi card in front of you
with no nigiri on it, that nigiri must be placed on top of the wasabi, and its
value is tripled:
   - Squid Nigiri on wasabi = 9 points
   - Salmon Nigiri on wasabi = 6 points
   - Egg Nigiri on wasabi = 3 points

You may have multiple wasabi cards in front of you, but only 1 nigiri card may
be placed on each wasabi. Because wasabi affects only the next nigiri played
after it, a nigiri you played before acquiring a wasabi is not tripled
retroactively. If you play a nigiri while you have no empty wasabi available,
that nigiri simply scores its normal (untripled) value. A wasabi that never
receives a nigiri scores nothing.

Chopsticks. A chopsticks card scores nothing on its own. Its purpose is to let
you take two cards on a later turn. Here is how it works: if you already have a
chopsticks card face-up in front of you (played on an earlier turn), then on a
future turn you may first choose one card from your hand as usual, and then,
before everyone else reveals their cards, call out "Sushi Go!" and take a
second card from your hand, also placing it face-down. All players then reveal
their cards; you reveal both of yours. Before passing hands, you put the
chopsticks card back into your hand, so it is passed on with the rest of your
hand and other players may take it and use it again.

Chopsticks limitations and edge cases:
   - You cannot play a chopsticks card and use it on the same turn; it must
     already be in front of you from a previous turn.
   - You may have multiple chopsticks cards in front of you, but you may use
     only 1 per turn.
   - Using chopsticks requires at least two cards in hand to take. On the final
     turn of a round, each hand holds only a single card, so chopsticks cannot
     be used then.

Pudding. Pudding cards are not scored during rounds. They are kept in front of
you (not discarded) and scored only once, at the very end of the game (see End
of Round / End of Game and Scoring).


END OF ROUND / END OF GAME

Ending a round. When the last card of each hand has been passed on and placed
face-up, the round is over. Score all collected cards according to the rules
above, and then:

1. Announce your round score for the scorekeeper to record.

2. Discard all of your scored cards face-up in a discard pile beside the
   central draw pile. The only exception is pudding cards: keep every pudding
   card in front of you, unscored, to be tallied at the end of the game.

3. Deal every player a new hand from the central draw pile, equal in size to
   that round's hand (10/9/8/7 cards for 2/3/4/5 players, the same as the
   previous round).

Then play the next round exactly as before. Discarded cards do not return to
the draw pile; the deck of 108 cards is large enough that the draw pile never
runs out across all three rounds for any supported player count.

Ending the game. After the third round has been scored, some cards will remain
in the central draw pile. These leftover cards are simply ignored. Now score
the puddings (see Scoring). Add each player's three round scores plus their
pudding result to get their final total.


SCORING AND TIEBREAKERS

Maki Rolls (end of each round). Compare the total number of maki-roll icons
each player collected.
   - The player with the most icons scores 6 points.
   - The player with the second most icons scores 3 points.
   - If two or more players tie for most, they split the 6 points evenly,
     ignoring any remainder, and no second-place points are awarded that round.
   - If two or more players tie for second, they split the 3 points evenly,
     ignoring any remainder.
   - Example: Chris has 5 maki icons, Phil has 3, Amy has 3, and Lisa has 2.
     Chris has the most and scores 6. Phil and Amy tie for second and split the
     3 points, scoring 1 each (remainder ignored). Lisa scores nothing.

Tempura, Sashimi, Dumplings, Nigiri, Wasabi, Chopsticks (end of each round).
Scored as described under Actions and Card Effects. These are individual to
each player, so there are no player-versus-player ties to break.

Puddings (end of the game only).
   - The player with the most pudding cards scores 6 points.
   - The player with the fewest pudding cards, including any player who has
     none, loses 6 points.
   - If two or more players tie for most, they split the 6 points evenly,
     ignoring any remainder.
   - If two or more players tie for least, they split the lost 6 points evenly,
     ignoring any remainder (each loses their share).
   - If all players have the same number of pudding cards, no one gains or
     loses any points for pudding.
   - Example: Chris has 4 puddings, Phil has 3, Lisa and Amy each have 0. Chris
     has the most and scores 6. Lisa and Amy tie for the least and split the
     -6, each losing 3.
   - 2-player exception: In a 2-player game, no one loses points for having the
     fewest puddings. Only the +6 for having the most pudding cards is awarded.

Winning. After all three rounds and pudding scoring, the player with the
highest total score wins. If two or more players tie on total score, the tie is
broken in favor of whoever has the most pudding cards.


HIDDEN INFORMATION

- Your hand is held in your hands and kept secret from all opponents. No one
  may look at another player's hand.

- Chosen cards are placed face-down each turn and remain hidden until the
  simultaneous reveal, so no one knows what any other player picked until
  everyone reveals at once.

- Collected (revealed) cards in each player's play area are face-up and fully
  visible to everyone for the rest of the round. Pudding cards kept between
  rounds are likewise face-up and visible to all.

- The central draw pile is face-down; its contents and order are unknown to
  everyone. The discard pile beside it is face-up.

- Because hands are passed around, over the course of a round you will see (and
  be able to remember) cards that pass through your hand, but you cannot see
  cards currently held by other players.


CLARIFICATIONS

- Base-game player counts. The standard game supports 2, 3, 4, and 5 players,
  all using the same core loop (choose a card, reveal simultaneously, pass the
  whole remaining hand to the left, three rounds). The 2-player game uses this
  same core loop with 10-card hands and the pudding "least" penalty removed, as
  stated in Scoring. (The rulebook also describes optional alternative ways to
  play, but those are not part of this core ruleset and are excluded here.)

- [UNCLEAR: Maki second place when the runner-up total is zero.] The rules say
  the player with the second-most maki icons scores 3 points, but do not state
  whether a player must have at least 1 maki icon to qualify. Options
  considered: (a) any player who is strictly second, even with 0 icons, scores
  the 3 points; (b) a player with 0 maki icons cannot place second and the 3
  points go unawarded (or are shared only among players who have at least 1
  icon). Note the rulebook is explicit that 0 counts as "least" for pudding,
  but gives no equivalent statement for maki.

- [UNCLEAR: Maki when no one has any maki icons.] If no player collected any
  maki cards in a round, the rulebook does not say whether the 6 and 3 points
  are awarded, split, or skipped. Assumed intent is that no maki points are
  awarded that round, but this is not stated.

- [UNCLEAR: Overall tie that is also a pudding tie.] The winner is decided by
  most pudding cards in the event of a points tie. The rules do not specify how
  to resolve a tie when the tied players also have the same number of pudding
  cards. Options considered: (a) declare a shared victory; (b) play a further
  tiebreaker of the group's choosing. The rulebook provides no default.

- Order of nigiri and wasabi. Wasabi affects only the next nigiri placed after
  it. If a player deliberately plays nigiri before acquiring a wasabi, that
  nigiri is not tripled; this is treated as intended behavior, not an error.
